05 — STEELMANNING THE OPPOSITION

The Counter-Argument

Every axiom deserves a worthy adversary. Toggle between the two schools of thought and judge the tradeoff yourself.

POSITION

Embrace failure to build resilience.

Systems should be hardened by deliberately injecting faults in production — kill nodes, throttle networks, corrupt packets — so engineers are forced to design for the chaos that will inevitably arrive uninvited.

KORE'S REBUTTAL

Chaos testing is a symptom of architectures that cannot guarantee their own behavior. It is a discipline for coping with entropy — Kore's position is to remove the entropy at the source instead.

POSITION

Eliminate uncertainty before it exists.

If every allocation, every scheduling decision, and every network hop is resolved deterministically at compile time, there is no chaos left to be resilient against — the failure modes are proven closed, not merely survived.

THE HONEST TRADEOFF

Determinism demands upfront rigor: every dependency must be known and bounded ahead of time. It trades runtime flexibility for compile-time proof — a fair price, in our view, for zero-surprise production systems.
